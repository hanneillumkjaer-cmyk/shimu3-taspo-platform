From Europe to the classroom: how donated equipment reaches Tanzanian schools

A donated whiteboard doesn't simply appear in a classroom in Tanzania. Here's the journey, and why careful logistics make every donation count.

When someone in Europe donates a whiteboard, a box of markers or a working laptop to TASPO, it's easy to imagine it simply appearing in a Tanzanian classroom a few days later. The reality involves quite a lot more.

Behind every delivery is a chain of practical steps: sourcing, packing, shipping, paperwork, customs and, finally, a delivery to a school that is ready and waiting. Getting each step right is what makes sure a donation ends up being used, rather than stuck in a warehouse or a port.

Step 1: Starting with what schools need

Everything starts in Tanzania, not in Europe. We work with the schools in our programmes to understand what they need, how many classrooms and pupils are involved, and what equipment will make the biggest difference.

That's why we always ask donors to contact us before sending anything. Well-meant donations that don't match a real need take up space and shipping budget that could have gone to something that's needed.

Step 2: Sourcing and collecting in Europe

Once we know what's needed, we work with our European partners and donors to find it. Sometimes that means collecting donated equipment. At other times, it means buying items with donated funds, which can be more efficient when we need a lot of the same thing, such as whiteboards and markers.

We check donated equipment carefully before it goes any further. Items need to be in good condition and useful in a Tanzanian classroom. IT equipment, for example, must work properly and come with the cables and chargers it needs.

Step 3: Packing and shipping

Equipment is packed to survive a long journey, then shipped to Tanzania. Bundling items together into larger shipments keeps costs down, so we plan carefully and ship when we have enough to make the journey worthwhile.

Good logistics are invisible when they work. You only notice them when they don't.

Step 4: Paperwork and customs

This is the step most people never think about, and one of the most important. Every shipment needs accurate documentation describing what's in it, its value and its purpose. Customs clearance in Tanzania has to be handled correctly, or a shipment can be delayed for weeks and pick up extra costs.

Careful preparation at this stage protects both the donation and the donor's goodwill.

Step 5: Delivery to the classroom

Once cleared, the equipment is delivered to the schools. Our team in Tanzania makes sure it reaches the right classrooms, and for projects such as Whiteboards for All, teachers get a short introduction to using and caring for their new equipment.

Step 6: Following up

A delivery isn't the end of the story. We follow up with schools to see how the equipment is being used and whether anything else is needed. We also share what we learn with our partners and donors, so they can see what their support achieved.

Want to help?

If you, your company or your organisation have equipment to donate, or would like to help fund shipping and customs costs, which is one of the most valuable and least glamorous ways to help, we'd love to talk.
